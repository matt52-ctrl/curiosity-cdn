memory

Knowing where to find information makes us forget it.

In a 2011 experiment, participants who believed a computer would save their typed facts remembered about a third less of the information compared to those who thought it would be erased.

The 2011 study by Betsy Sparrow et al. showed a drop from a 29% recall rate (erased group) to 19% (saved group), representing a ~34% relative decrease, not 40%. Additionally, the effect has failed to replicate in multiple preregistered replication attempts.

We do not store the answer; we store the map to the answer.
