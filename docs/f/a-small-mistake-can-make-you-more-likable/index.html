A small mistake can make you more likable.

In a 1966 experiment, the 'pratfall effect' demonstrated that highly competent individuals are rated more favorably after a minor social blunder, while the same blunder causes those perceived as mediocre to be rated less favorably.

The study shows that the impact of a blunder on interpersonal attraction is strictly moderated by the target's initial level of perceived competence.

You might be more attractive when you are not perfect.

Where this comes from

Study
Aronson, Willerman, and Floyd (1966)
What the check found
The original 1966 study shows the 'pratfall effect' is conditional; the blunder only increases attractiveness if the person is already perceived as highly competent.
Confidence
Well replicated
