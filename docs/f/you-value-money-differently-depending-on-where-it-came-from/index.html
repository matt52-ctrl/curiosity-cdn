You value money differently depending on where it came from.

A 1985 study found that people treat funds differently—and are more likely to spend 'windfall' gains—even when the amount is logically identical to their earned income.

Mental accounting creates psychological compartments for money. We assign different labels, such as 'savings', 'disposable', or 'bonus', which govern our spending habits more strongly than the total value of the money itself.

Your bank balance is one number, but your brain processes it as a dozen separate budgets.
