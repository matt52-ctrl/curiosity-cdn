decision making

Having too many choices makes us buy nothing.

A 2000 study found that while 24 choices of jam attracted more onlookers, only 3% bought any, compared to 30% when offered only six choices.

Researchers set up a tasting booth in a gourmet market, alternating between six and 24 flavors of jam. While the larger display drew more crowds, the smaller display led to ten times more sales.

Your difficulty choosing a movie on streaming sites is a predictable reaction to abundance.

Where this comes from

Study
Iyengar & Lepper, 2000, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The 2000 study by Iyengar & Lepper indeed found that 3% (4 of 145) of shoppers who stopped at the 24-jam display purchased jam, compared to 30% (31 of 104) at the 6-jam display. Note that later meta-analyses have struggled to replicate this choice-overload effect consistently.
