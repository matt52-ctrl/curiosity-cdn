affect heuristic

You assess risks as lower because you feel good.

A 2000 study demonstrated that affective evaluation of an activity, influenced by information framing, causes inverse shifts in perceived risk and benefit.

The study utilized information manipulation to influence affect towards specific activities rather than inducing emotional states like joy or sadness.

You view the world through the lens of your current mood.

Where this comes from

Study
Slovic, Finucane, Peters, and MacGregor, 2000, Journal of Behavioral Decision Making
What the check found
The study confirmed the affect heuristic (risk/benefit correlation) but did not induce transient emotional states (joy/sadness) in participants. It manipulated perceptions of specific activities via descriptive text.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
