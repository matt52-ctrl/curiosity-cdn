social dynamics

A small favor makes a large request more likely.

A 1966 study found that people who agreed to a trivial request were significantly more likely to agree to a larger, related request a few days later.

The original study conducted by Freedman and Fraser utilized a timeframe of a few days, not two weeks.

Once you agree to a small initial term, your identity begins to shift to accommodate the commitment.

Where this comes from

Study
Freedman and Fraser, 1966, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The study occurred, but the timeframe is misstated as two weeks rather than a few days. The attribution to Freedman and Fraser (1966) is otherwise correct.
Confidence
Solid, with limits
