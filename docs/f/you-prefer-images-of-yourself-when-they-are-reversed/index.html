You prefer images of yourself when they are reversed.

A 1977 study showed that people prefer their own mirror image, while their friends prefer the standard photographic version.

We see our own faces in the mirror daily, creating a familiarity bias toward that specific orientation. Because faces are not perfectly symmetrical, a standard photograph shows a left-right reversal that feels inherently "wrong" to us. Your friends, who rarely look at you in a mirror, perceive the true, non-reversed orientation as correct.

You are essentially a stranger to your own photograph.

Where this comes from

Study
Mita, Dermer, and Knight, 1977, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The study by Mita, Dermer, and Knight (1977), 'Reversed facial images and the mere-exposure hypothesis', confirms that subjects prefer their own mirror image while friends prefer the standard non-reversed photograph.
