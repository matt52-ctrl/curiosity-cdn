decision making

You feel more in control when you participate in luck.

In a 1975 study, participants perceived a game based entirely on chance as a skill-based challenge simply because they were allowed to choose their own lottery ticket.

Ellen Langer found that when people make active choices in a random environment—like picking a number or rolling dice themselves—they overestimate their probability of winning. The illusion of control stems from the mind conflating agency with outcome probability.

You are often convinced that your specific rituals influence outcomes that are statistically indifferent to your actions.

Where this comes from

Study
Ellen Langer, 1975, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
What the check found
The study (Langer, 1975, JPSP) confirms that participants who choose their own ticket (choice condition) demonstrate a higher expectancy of success than those assigned one, confirming the illusion of control.
