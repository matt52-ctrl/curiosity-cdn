Ease of memory leads us to overestimate rare events.

A 1973 study showed that people judge the frequency of an event by how easily they can recall examples from memory.

Because dramatic or scary events are easier to remember, they seem more frequent than common, mundane ones. The brain mistakes the vividness of an image for the probability of its occurrence.

If you can imagine it clearly, you fear it disproportionately.
