cognitive bias

The first number you hear dictates your future estimates.

A 1974 study showed that initial, irrelevant numbers significantly bias subsequent numerical estimations.

When participants were shown a wheel of fortune, their guesses for the number of nations in the UN clustered near the random number generated by the wheel. The brain uses the first available datum as a cognitive reference point for the entire problem. Your estimates are rarely as objective as you assume.

You are never making a judgment from a blank slate.

Where this comes from

Study
Tversky and Kahneman (1974)
What the check found
The 1974 Science paper 'Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases' is correctly attributed and describes the wheel of fortune experiment as a foundational demonstration of the anchoring heuristic.
